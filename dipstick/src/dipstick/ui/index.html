Dipstick

Dipstick sends commands to the NanoVNA. Pick only your NanoVNA: sent to a radio's cable, they could change the radio's settings. Dipstick remembers the choice and will treat any device with the same USB ID as a NanoVNA.

Remembered USB ID:

Sweeps from 1 MHz up to 200 MHz (how high depends on Longest cable), outside the ham bands. Take the antenna off and leave the cable's far end open or shorted. Give the velocity factor to measure the length, or the length to measure the velocity factor.

Tests an unun or voltage balun on its own. Take the antenna wire off, put a resistor of 50 times the ratio across its output with short leads (2450 ohms for a 49:1, 50 ohms for a 1:1), and connect its input to CH0. Leave Resistor empty if yours is exactly that, or type what you used. Sweeps 1.8 to 54 MHz, outside the ham bands. Never connect a radio to the NanoVNA: its ports take milliwatts.

Tests how well a current balun (a choke) blocks current on the outside of the coax. Calibrate first on the Calibrate tab: HF wide, with Both ports ticked. Take the balun off the antenna and the coax. Join its two input terminals (an SO-239's centre pin and shell) and wire them to CH0's centre pin; join its two output terminals and wire them to CH1's centre pin; then connect the outer shells of CH0 and CH1 with a short wire. Keep every wire short. Sweeps 1.8 to 54 MHz, outside the ham bands. Never connect a radio to the NanoVNA: its ports take milliwatts.

Measures how much signal is lost passing through. Calibrate first on the Calibrate tab: HF wide, with Both ports ticked. One item, direct: for a 1:1 balun, a cable or a filter. Its input goes to CH0 and its output to CH1 (a balun's two output terminals to CH1's centre pin and shell, with short leads). Two transformers back to back: for a pair of identical transformers, such as two 49:1s. Join their high-impedance sides terminal to terminal with short wires, then one's coax side to CH0 and the other's to CH1. Sweeps 1.8 to 54 MHz, outside the ham bands. Never connect a radio to the NanoVNA: its ports take milliwatts.

Calibrating shows the NanoVNA what its own connector and cable look like, so it can take them out of every measurement. Use the calibration kit that came with it (open, short and 50-ohm load), screwed onto CH0, the port you measure antennas on, with any adapter or jumper you'll measure through. The new calibration covers only the band (or HF wide range) you choose, and isn't saved in the NanoVNA. Tick Both ports to add two steps for CH1, the port that measures what gets through something.
